Climate Change and Adaptation

Advancing Africa's Climate Response

Research on what a changing climate is already doing to livelihoods, which adaptation measures hold up under scrutiny, and how climate finance and policy can be made to reach the people carrying the most risk.

Why this matters

Climate change is not a future scenario for the communities we work with. It is a present change in the conditions their livelihoods were built around.

The concentration of atmospheric carbon dioxide exceeded 414 parts per million in 2019. Without drastic, concerted and urgent action it is likely that the earth will experience a temperature rise of up to 1.5 °C relative to pre-industrial averages by 2100.

Those numbers mean little to nothing among pastoralists, fisherfolk and small-scale farmer groups who are already experiencing rapid societal and livelihood changes driven by climate variability alongside other confounding factors. The lived signal arrives as a failed season, a moved grazing route or a source that no longer holds through the dry months.

This is why our climate research is grounded in specific systems — water, energy, food — rather than treated as a separate sector. Adaptation is rarely a climate project; it is a water supply that tolerates variability, a crop calendar that matches shifted rainfall, or an energy system that keeps working through a flood.

Finance is the persistent bottleneck. Adaptation is chronically underfunded relative to mitigation, and locally led adaptation more so again. Understanding how climate finance is structured, appraised and disbursed is inseparable from understanding whether adaptation actually happens.

How we work

Our climate research pairs empirical field evidence with policy and financial analysis.

Household surveys

Structured data collection sampled to support statements about a population rather than anecdotes about a site, covering service levels, expenditure and coping behaviour.

Mixed-methods evaluation

Quantitative measurement paired with interviews and focus groups, so a result arrives with an explanation of the mechanism behind it.

Policy and regulatory analysis

Reading strategies, standards and regulations against one another to locate the contradictions, gaps and unfunded mandates that stall implementation.

Climate risk and vulnerability analysis

Assessment of exposure and sensitivity for specific livelihoods and systems, kept at a resolution that can inform an actual planning decision.

Climate finance tracking

Tracing flows and instruments to establish what has been committed, what has been disbursed and what reached local level — three quantities that routinely differ.

Geospatial and scenario analysis

Spatial and scenario methods used to examine how projected change intersects with settlement, water availability and land use.
