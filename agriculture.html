Research Pillar 04

Agriculture and Food Systems

Research on the livelihoods of smallholder farmers under rising climate pressure, and on the water, land and market systems that determine whether their production is viable.

Why this matters

Agriculture is how most households in sub-Saharan Africa live. It is also the sector most directly exposed to climate variability.

Agriculture is the main source of livelihood for most households in sub-Saharan Africa, employing more than 60% of the total population and contributing about 23% of the region’s total Gross Domestic Product. It is not a peripheral sector; it is the economic base.

It is also almost entirely rainfed. Only 6% of farmland in the region has access to irrigation, which means the sector’s output is tied directly to rainfall in a period when rainfall is becoming less predictable. The region’s 33 million smallholder farmers carry that exposure with very limited buffers.

That combination — high economic dependence, minimal irrigation and rising variability — is why we treat agriculture as a water and climate question as much as an agronomic one. The productive constraint is frequently not the seed or the practice but the reliability of water and the absence of any mechanism to absorb a bad season.

Markets complete the picture. Yield gains that cannot be stored, moved or sold do not translate into income, so our agricultural research follows the chain from field conditions through post-harvest handling to the terms on which smallholders reach a buyer.

What we work on

Four research streams within the agriculture pillar.

01

Smallholder productivity and climate risk

How climate variability transmits into yields and incomes for smallholder producers, and which practices, inputs and risk instruments hold up under the constraints smallholders actually face.

Productivity
02

Irrigation and water for agriculture

Small-scale irrigation viability, water allocation between agricultural and domestic use, and the energy requirements of pumping — where this pillar meets the WASH and energy pillars directly.

Water
03

Land use and land-use change

Competition between cropping, grazing, settlement and conservation, and the land-tenure conditions that shape whether long-horizon investment in productivity is rational.

Land
04

Food systems and value chains

Post-harvest loss, processing, cold chain and market access, and the terms of trade that decide how much of a crop’s final value returns to the person who grew it.

Value chains

How we work

Our agricultural research is field-based and follows systems across sectoral boundaries.

Household surveys

Structured data collection sampled to support statements about a population rather than anecdotes about a site, covering service levels, expenditure and coping behaviour.

Mixed-methods evaluation

Quantitative measurement paired with interviews and focus groups, so a result arrives with an explanation of the mechanism behind it.

Policy and regulatory analysis

Reading strategies, standards and regulations against one another to locate the contradictions, gaps and unfunded mandates that stall implementation.

Farm and household-level assessment

Production, input use, labour and income data collected at the level where agricultural decisions are actually made.

Value chain analysis

Tracing a commodity from field to buyer to establish where value accrues, where losses occur and which actors hold the leverage.

Geospatial and land-use analysis

Spatial analysis of cropping, water availability and land-use change, used to examine pressure and competition over time.
